# Negotiation framework (for brand deals)

**Captured:** 2026-10-26 (Sandbox guest session this week).  
**Use:** prepare, run, and debrief sponsorship negotiations — rate cards, term sheets-lite,
usage rights, and “agency vs creator team” talks with brands.

Definition used in the talk: negotiation = any attempt to persuade or influence another party.
Sales and brand deals are negotiations inside ongoing relationships, not one-off transactions.

## Assumptions to drop

- It’s a fixed pie / win by beating the other side.
- Everyone values the same things (they don’t — look for complementary interests).
- Power and leverage always decide the outcome.
- People forget being taken advantage of (they don’t).

## Seven elements

Use these to **prepare**, **conduct**, and **debrief**. Graphically, most value sits in the
middle circle: **interests · options · criteria**. Fighting over BATNAs or pounding a number is
weaker.

| # | Element | Meaning | Brand-deal example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Alternatives (BATNA)** | Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement — what you do if you walk | Another brand at a similar rate; organic content week; for the brand: another creator. Walk if the deal is worse than your BATNA. |
| 2 | **Interests** | Hopes, fears, desires — yours, theirs, and third parties | Creator: cash, fit, control, time. Brand: performance, usage, timeline, category exclusivity. Third parties: audience trust, other managed creators who share referrals. |
| 3 | **Options** | Possible pieces of a deal with this party (not walkaways) | Flat rate vs views; short vs long-form; usage window; exclusivity; multi-platform bundle; referral to another creator on the roster. Treat as a **basket**, not issue-by-issue. |
| 4 | **Criteria** | Standards independent of either side’s wish | Peer rates by niche/size; what we closed last month for similar asks; “do you want more than is fair?” Prefer arguing standards over haggling a naked number. |
| 5 | **Commitment** | Clear oral/written who does what when | Signed rate, deliverables, due dates, usage, payment terms. Get promises in writing — especially with people you like. |
| 6 | **Communication** | Listening > broadcasting; agenda; questions | Open with inquiry, not advocacy. Bring a short agenda; people almost never have one. Listen for facts that change your assumptions. |
| 7 | **Relationship** | Can you work through differences over time? | Brands and creators you’ll see again. How you negotiate is evidence of whether the partnership will work. |

## Habits to keep

1. **Have a framework** (this one, or a one-page version on the deal card).
2. **Prepare** every important deal — if you only take one thing: prepare. The other side usually doesn’t.
3. **Debrief** with yourself or [Partner]: what worked, what we’d do differently.
4. **See it as joint problem-solving** — expand the pie (extra platform, longer usage for more fee, referral) instead of only splitting cash.
5. **Practice low-stakes** and log negotiations for a week (any persuasion counts). Reps beat anxiety.

## Maps onto our agency bet

From [`problem-bet.md`](problem-bet.md) and [`customer-avatars.md`](customer-avatars.md):

- **Breakout core buyer** fails on admin and gut-feel rates → we bring **criteria** (rate card) and **commitment** (tracker stages, payment dates).
- **Undercharging** is a criteria failure (“random” $300 vs peer ~$800).
- **Brand referral pool** is an **option** that answers “I can do this myself.”
- **Walk away / lost after 3 follows** is explicit BATNA hygiene (already how we mark lost deals).
- Prefer **flat rate** (Creator R, Creator E language) — avoids view-based traps; still use criteria to defend the number.

## Agenda starter (brand or creator)

1. Interests (ours / theirs — list assumptions, invite correction).
2. Options basket (rate, formats, usage, timeline, extras).
3. Criteria (comps, last closes, floors on the rate card).
4. Alternatives if we don’t agree.
5. Commitments and next written step.
6. Open questions (listen first).
