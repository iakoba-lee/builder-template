## What I asked / what happened

Background: film school, then TV camera operator, then on-air tech presenter for a shopping
channel and a news channel, then TikTok and YouTube full time. They split time between Australia
and the UK. I asked how they handle sponsorships today.

## What they actually do today

- Receives roughly 50 sponsorship emails a week, "most of them are from like really spammy
  Chinese or whatever random apps." Many land in spam.
- Reads them one by one each morning, researches the brand, and decides whether it fits.
- Uses an AI tool, Instinct, that summarizes the inbox and drafts replies. "That's been really
  useful."
- On a trial with a new Australian agency for tech creators. The agency has sent two deals so
  far. In that model the creator forwards emails they like, and the agency negotiates.
- Turned down a UK agency that wanted to create a new email address and run everything, because
  that gives "a bit less control, especially if you want to leave."
- Brand relationships come mostly through each brand's local PR firm. They review some devices
  for free to stay on good terms with PR contacts.

## Pains

- **Inbox triage eats the morning.** "I'll just go through them one by one and they'll take all
  morning to go through. It's ridiculous." Cost: about half their time, by their estimate.
- **Writing emails.** "I hate writing emails."
- **Undercharging.** For a long-form video from a Chinese brand they asked "a grand or two." The
  new agency says five to ten thousand. Cost: possibly thousands of dollars per deal.
- **Brands moving the conversation to WhatsApp.** "I do not want to be talking to random Chinese
  brand people on WhatsApp."
- **Off-niche deals cause trouble later.** One well-paid pool-cleaner deal brought in a stream of
  pool-cleaner offers. Ergonomic chairs: "never doing that again… nobody cares about that
  content."
- **No TikTok creator payouts in Australia**, so sponsorships are their only short-form income.
- **Travel to trade shows is costly** (about US$2–3k in flights for CES) unless a brand pays for
  it.

## Solutions they've tried

| Tried | Still using? | Why it fell short |
|---|---|---|
| Handling the inbox by hand | Yes | Takes all morning; they hate it |
| Instinct (AI inbox summaries and replies) | Yes | Didn't say it fell short |
| Australian tech-creator agency (trial) | Yes, on trial | Too early; two deals so far |
| UK agency that runs a new email address | Declined | Less control; risk of losing contacts if they leave |
| Free device reviews for PR goodwill | Yes | Gets access, but the products can be weak |

## Strongest signal

**They didn't know they were undercharging until an agency told them,** by a factor of about
three to five. "How much do I charge?" is, in their words, the question everyone asks. They also
said that never crossed their mind until a fellow creator brought their agent to lunch.
