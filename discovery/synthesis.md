## The main finding: there are two kinds of creator with different problems

### 1. Creators who have never had a deal: they don't know the steps, so they don't start

Kimball AI, Creator C, and Creator J all had an audience. C and J also had brand emails sitting
in their inboxes. None of them had acted.

- No work email, or no email in the bio, so brands had no way to reach them (Kimball AI,
  Creator J).
- Brand emails left unopened or unanswered (Creator C: Hollyland, Anker; Creator J: "a handful").
- No idea of price, packages, or how payment works.
- This happens even to people with business skills. Creator J runs his own marketing agency and
  still said, "I just don't know the game on how it works."

**The pain is not "I'm too busy." It's "I don't know what I'm supposed to do."**

### 2. Creators who already get deals: the admin swamps them, and they often don't notice

My partner and Creator D.

- Inbox triage takes hours. Creator D: "they'll take all morning to go through."
- Missed deadlines and slow replies damage brand relationships (my partner, before anyone helped
  him).
- **They often can't see it.** My partner needed his spouse to tell him. Creator D only learned
  they were undercharging, by about three to five times, when an agency told them.
- A helper with limited time doesn't last. My partner's spouse fixed the problem until she got
  busy.

## What this means

**For content (the reason this sprint was run):**
1. **Lead with pricing.** It's the only pain all four creators who discussed it raised, beginners
   and experienced alike. "How much should a creator with N followers charge?" is a ready-made
   series.
2. **Make a beginner's checklist:** work email, email in bio, matching profiles, a package menu
   (short-form, long-form, mid-roll, usage rights), and a rate range. Three of five didn't have
   these.
3. **Describe symptoms, not services.** People with the inbox problem don't call it "sponsorship
   management." Name missed deadlines, unanswered brand emails, and brands that stop replying.

**For the agency:** the two types of creator need different offers. Beginners need setup and
coaching (the free/paid "school" idea from the 2026-09-23 debrief). Busy creators need someone to
take over the inbox.

**For the product in this repo:** **no one raised link-in-bio pages or click analytics.** The
closest point was "put your email in your bio," which is about how brands reach a creator, not
analytics. That doesn't mean the link page is wrong; nobody was asked about it. But nothing this
sprint shows creators asking for it, which is consistent with `personas.md` (they have never paid
about $5/month for click data). Bring this to sprint 3 planning before building more there.

## Brands

One brand conversation, brief and unrecorded, so treat this as a lead, not a finding.

- The brand pays agencies a lot to run creator campaigns, and communication with those agencies
  has been poor.
- Now that we're connected directly, they said they'll work with us directly instead.
- **This matches the creator side.** Creators lose deals by replying slowly and missing
  deadlines. The brand's complaint about its agencies is also about communication. On both sides,
  responsiveness looks like the thing being paid for.
- **A risk to check:** if brands dislike "agencies" in general, presenting ourselves as an agency
  could hurt us. "The creator's team" may land better. Ask the next brand.
