# Interview Guide — Sprint 2

**What this sprint is trying to learn:** what creators struggle with in getting sponsorships,
what they have already tried to fix it, and the exact words they use to describe it. See
`sprints/sprint-2-plan.md`.

This is a guide, not a script. Have a conversation, follow what is interesting, and use the
questions below when it stalls.

## Rules for every interview

Adapted from *The Mom Test* (`builder-resources/discovery.md`).

- **Ask about the past, not the future.** "Tell me about the last time…" gets you facts.
  "Would you…?" gets you polite guesses.
- **Don't pitch.** Don't mention the agency's services, and don't say what you are building.
  Once they know what you're selling, everything they say bends toward it. If they ask, say
  you'll tell them at the end, and then do.
- **Go after specifics.** When they say "it's a pain," ask "when did that last happen?" and
  "what did you do about it?"
- **Find out what it cost them.** Time, money, deals lost. A problem they have never spent
  anything on is a weaker problem than one they have paid to fix.
- **Write down their words, not your summary.** The phrases they use are one of the deliverables.
  If they say "brands ghost me," write down "brands ghost me," not "communication issues."
- **Talk less than a third of the time.** Silence is fine. People usually fill it with the
  interesting part.

## Before the call

- [ ] Ask permission to record: "Mind if I record this so I can listen instead of taking
      notes? It stays with me, and anything I write up won't use your name."
- [ ] Know which group they're in (below) and pick that section's questions.
- [ ] Have `interviews/000-interview-template.md` open for right after.

## Opening (all groups, 2–3 min)

- "Thanks for doing this. I'm trying to understand how creators actually handle sponsorships:
  what works, what doesn't. There are no right answers, and nothing you say will hurt my
  feelings."
- "Give me the quick version of your channel. What you make, where, and how long you've been
  at it."
- "Roughly how much of your income comes from sponsorships right now, compared with ads,
  affiliate links, or anything else?"

## Group A — Creators who handle sponsorships themselves

- "Tell me about the last sponsorship you landed. How did it start?"
  - Who reached out first? How did they find you, or you them?
  - What happened between first contact and getting paid? Walk me through it.
- "Tell me about one that fell through or that you turned down. What happened?"
- "In a typical month, how much time goes into sponsorships: finding brands, emailing,
  negotiating, reporting?"
- "What do you send a brand when they ask about your audience or your rates? How do you put
  that together?"
- "How did you decide what to charge the last time?"
- "What have you tried to make this easier?" (tools, templates, platforms, marketplaces)
  - "Why did you stop using it?" / "What's still annoying about it?"
- "Have you ever thought about getting help with this: a manager or an agency? What happened?"

## Group B — Creators who hired a person (manager, assistant, VA)

- "What were you doing yourself before you hired them? What made you decide to get help?"
- "How did you find them? What did you look for?"
- "What do they handle now, and what do you still do yourself?"
- "How do you pay them?" (flat fee, percentage, hourly) "How did you land on that?"
- "What's better than before? Can you give me an example?"
- "What still goes wrong, or what do you wish they did differently?"
- "Have you ever thought about switching or going back to doing it yourself? Why?"

## Group C — Creators who hired an agency

- Same as Group B, plus:
- "Why an agency, not one person?"
- "How do you know whether the agency is doing a good job? What do they show you?"
- "Tell me about a moment you were frustrated with them."
- "If you left the agency tomorrow, what would you miss? What wouldn't you miss?"

## Group D — The agency's current clients

**Bias warning:** they work with you, so they will lean toward being nice. Push harder for
specifics and ask for the negative directly. Consider whether your partner, or someone outside
the agency, should run these instead.

- "Take me back to before we started working together. What was sponsorship like then?
  Tell me about a specific deal from that time."
- "What made you decide to bring in help?"
- "What's actually different now? Give me an example from the last month."
- "What's still hard, even with us?"
- "What's one thing we do that you'd drop if you could, or that you don't see the point of?"
- "If a creator friend asked you whether to hire an agency, what would you tell them?"
  (Write this one down word for word. It's how they describe the value to someone else.)

## Group E — Brands (marketing, partnerships, or influencer managers)

- "Tell me about the last creator campaign you ran. How did you pick the creators?"
- "Where do you find creators? What makes you reach out to one and skip another?"
- "What do you ask a creator for before you sign? What do you actually look at?"
  (media kit, demographics, past results, rates)
- "Tell me about a campaign that went badly. What happened?"
- "When you work through an agency or manager instead of the creator directly, what's
  different, better or worse?"
- "After a campaign, how do you decide whether it worked? What numbers do you need from the
  creator?"
- "How do you decide what to pay?"

## Closing (all groups, 3–5 min)

- "Is there anything about this I should have asked and didn't?"
- "Who else should I talk to? Another creator, a manager, a brand contact?"
  (This is how you get from 3 interviews to 10.)
- "Can I follow up if I have a question later?"
- Now you can say what the agency does, if they asked.
